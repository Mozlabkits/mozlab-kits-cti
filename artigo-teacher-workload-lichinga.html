This qualitative study examines the effects of teacher workload on teaching quality in two rural primary schools in Lichinga District, Niassa Province, Mozambique. The study employed a descriptive and interpretive qualitative approach, using semi-structured interviews, non-participant observation, and document analysis. Six participants were involved, including four primary school teachers and two school managers. The findings indicate that prolonged working hours, teacher shortages, unequal distribution of responsibilities, limited lesson preparation time, reduced individualized student feedback, and limited opportunities for pedagogical innovation were associated with increased workload and reduced teaching quality. The study argues that teacher workload should be understood not only as an individual challenge but also as a consequence of organizational and structural conditions affecting rural schools. The study recommends strengthening teacher recruitment and equitable deployment, reducing unnecessary administrative burdens, and increasing institutional support for rural schools in Mozambique.
